Alongside the general GitLaw Agent, GitLaw offers **specialist legal agents** - each tuned to a specific area of law and jurisdiction. Specialists draw on deeper, focused knowledge of the statutes and document types in their field, so you get sharper first drafts and more thorough reviews for that kind of work.

## General Agent vs specialists

|                 | General GitLaw Agent                           | Specialist agent                                                               |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Best for        | Any contract or legal question, broad coverage | A specific document type in a specific jurisdiction                            |
| Knowledge       | Broad, general legal drafting and review       | Focused on one area's statutes and document types                              |
| How it's chosen | Used by default                                | GitLaw brings one in automatically when it fits your document and jurisdiction |

You don't have to choose - or even think about - specialists. GitLaw automatically calls in the right specialist when one is available and useful for what you're working on, and falls back to the general Agent the rest of the time. There's nothing for you to switch on.

## How specialists get used

You don't pick or start a specialist yourself - it happens automatically. Just describe what you need to GitLaw in plain language, exactly as you normally would. When your document type and jurisdiction match an available specialist, GitLaw brings that specialist's focused knowledge to bear; otherwise the general Agent handles it. Either way it's the same chat, with nothing extra to set up.

💡 Always set your jurisdiction for the best results. See [Set your jurisdiction](/knowledge-base/set-your-jurisdiction).
