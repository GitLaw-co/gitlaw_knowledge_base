Comments in GitLaw are anchored to the exact text you highlight, kept in a separate layer so your document content is never modified when you annotate it. Threads can be replied to and resolved without being deleted.

### Add a comment

1. Select the text you want to annotate - this can span a single word, a clause, or multiple paragraphs.
2. A context menu or toolbar option will appear; click **Comment**.
3. Type your note and submit. The highlighted range gains a visual indicator in the document body.

Comments carry your name and a timestamp automatically.

### Reply to a thread

1. Click the comment indicator on the highlighted text (or find the thread in the comments panel).
2. Click **Reply** beneath the thread.
3. Type your reply and submit.

Replies are nested under the original comment, keeping the discussion in one place.

### Resolve a thread

Once a point is addressed, click **Resolve** on the thread. The thread collapses and is marked as done - it is not deleted, so you can review it later. Resolved threads remain visible in the comments panel when you filter for them.

👉 If a comment is attached to a tracked change, accepting or rejecting that change will automatically resolve the paired comment. See [Accept or reject tracked changes](/knowledge-base/accept-or-reject-tracked-changes).

### Comments on imported documents

When you import a Word (.docx) file, any existing comments are brought in and displayed with an **External** badge alongside the original author name. These imported comments are read-only records of the counterparty's annotations - GitLaw does not attempt to match them to a GitLaw user account.

### Exporting with comments

When you [export your document](/knowledge-base/export-your-document) to Word, open comments are included as standard Word comments, so recipients see them in their usual review pane. Resolved threads are not included in the export - they stay in GitLaw.

When you re-import an exported file (for example, after a counterparty has added their own notes), GitLaw matches the original comments back to avoid duplicates. Any new comments added by the counterparty are imported as External threads.

👉 For a full redlining workflow that combines comments with tracked changes, see [Negotiate and redline a contract](/knowledge-base/negotiate-and-redline-a-contract).
