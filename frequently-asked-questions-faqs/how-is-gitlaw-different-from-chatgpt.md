Both GitLaw and ChatGPT use AI to help with text, but they are built for very different jobs. ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant; GitLaw is a legal document platform where AI is one part of a wider workflow.

### What makes GitLaw different

| Capability                        | GitLaw                                                                                                         | General chatbot (eg. ChatGPT)                                  |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lawyer-vetted templates**       | Drafts from a library of verified templates (NDAs, service agreements, etc.), surfaced and ranked by the Agent | Generates text from scratch with no guaranteed legal grounding |
| **Structured legal reasoning**    | Applies your jurisdiction, deal context, and playbook to select clauses and flag deviations                    | No awareness of your specific context or playbook              |
| **Real document editor**          | Full-featured editor with tracked changes, inline comments, and DOCX export (Word-compatible)                  | Plain text output only - no editor, no formatting              |
| **AI-powered redlining**          | Reviews a contract and proposes edits as track changes; flags high-risk or non-standard clauses                | Cannot mark up a document or produce tracked changes           |
| **Negotiation support**           | Analyses a counterparty's redlines, summarises changes, and suggests counter-proposals                         | No concept of a negotiation cycle                              |
| **Version history & audit trail** | Every edit is versioned; full history is available for review                                                  | No document history                                            |
| **Contract organisation**         | Documents live in a structured repository alongside templates and collaborators                                | Output exists only in the chat window                          |

### In plain language

When you use a general chatbot for legal work, you get a block of text that you then have to paste somewhere, format yourself, share manually, and track changes on separately - if at all. With GitLaw, the draft goes straight into a proper editor, your colleague can redline it, the AI can review the counterparty's version, and the full history is preserved.

GitLaw is also honest about its limits. Verified templates carry a quality signal; where no high-confidence template exists, the Agent tells you and drafts from general knowledge instead. As with any AI tool, you should review the output and involve a qualified lawyer when the stakes require it.
