## The short answer

Inviting people is built into GitLaw - not locked behind an upgrade. Whenever someone else works on contracts with you, bring them in: **a colleague, a family member, or your outside lawyer or accountant.**

## Who you might invite

- **Your team** - colleagues who work on your business's contracts.
- **Your family** - in your personal organisation, maybe a partner sorting out a tenancy or family paperwork.
- **Your lawyer or accountant** - outside advisers who need to see specific files.

## They see shared files, not private ones

Every file is either **shared with your organisation** or **private** - you choose as you add it. People you invite see the organisation's shared files; private files stay yours. See [Can my teammates see or edit all my files?](/knowledge-base/can-teammates-see-or-edit-all-my-files)

## Working with people in another organisation

If the person you want to work with - an outside lawyer, say - has their own GitLaw organisation, you can share across organisations. **That's always free.** See [Do I pay for people I share with?](/knowledge-base/do-i-pay-for-people-i-share-with).

## Who can invite

Inviting and managing people depends on your role. See [Organisation roles and permissions, explained](/knowledge-base/organisation-roles-and-permissions-explained).
