No. GitLaw is a powerful tool for drafting and reviewing contracts, but it is not a substitute for a qualified lawyer, and it does not provide legal advice.

**What GitLaw does well:** GitLaw uses lawyer-vetted templates and structured AI reasoning to help you draft contracts, spot risky or non-standard clauses, and produce professional documents far more quickly than starting from a blank page. For routine, well-understood agreements - a mutual NDA, a standard service agreement, a simple employment contract - GitLaw can produce a solid first draft and flag obvious issues at a fraction of the cost of hiring an attorney for every document.

**What GitLaw does not do:** GitLaw does not give legal advice, does not form an attorney–client relationship, and is not a regulated legal services provider. The AI cannot fully account for your specific circumstances, local law nuances, recent case law, or the commercial dynamics of a particular deal. Output from GitLaw - however accurate it looks - is a starting point, not a final legal opinion.

#### When you should still talk to a lawyer

- **High-stakes matters** - fundraising documents, mergers and acquisitions, employment disputes, intellectual property assignments, or anything where getting it wrong could cost you significantly.
- **Novel or bespoke situations** - where standard templates do not fit your circumstances.
- **Regulated industries** - where specific statutory requirements or professional obligations apply.
- **Counterparty pushback** - when the other side has legal representation and you are negotiating material terms.
- **Any time you are unsure** - if a clause or concept is unfamiliar, ask a lawyer rather than relying solely on AI output.

#### A note on human review

GitLaw is designed to complement professional legal advice, not replace it. We are building a marketplace where you will be able to request review of an AI-generated document by a verified, practising lawyer directly within GitLaw - getting human validation at lower cost and faster turnaround than traditional legal services. In the meantime, the best practice is to use GitLaw to prepare a thorough first draft, then have a lawyer review it before signing anything consequential.
