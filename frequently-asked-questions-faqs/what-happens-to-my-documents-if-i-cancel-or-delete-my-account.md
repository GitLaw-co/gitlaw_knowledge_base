It depends on whether you are **cancelling your plan** or **deleting your account** - these are two very different actions.

### Cancelling or downgrading your plan

Cancelling a paid subscription does not remove your account or your documents. You move to the free plan, which comes with a reduced monthly AI credit allowance. Everything you have saved - documents, folders, and shared files - stays exactly where it is.

You can [export any document](/knowledge-base/export-your-document) at any time, whether you are on a paid or free plan. To understand what changes when you move between plans, see [How does billing work?](/knowledge-base/how-does-billing-work) or [How do I cancel or change my plan?](/knowledge-base/how-do-i-cancel-or-change-my-plan).

### Deleting your account

Account deletion permanently removes your personal data and your private documents. Before requesting deletion, export anything you want to keep - there is no way to recover documents after the process completes.

Here is what happens to documents that belong to an organisation or have collaborators:

- **Shared or public documents** - files you own that are shared with collaborators, or that are public, are transferred to GitLaw's organisation so that collaborators retain access. They are not deleted.
- **Organisation-linked documents** - files associated with an organisation account are reassigned to that organisation. Collaborators within the organisation continue to have access under the same permissions.
- **Private documents with no collaborators** - these belong to you alone and are removed along with your account data.

Your personal profile, account settings, and any user-scoped data are deleted. Documents that others depend on are preserved under the appropriate organisation or collaborator ownership.

> **Before you request deletion:** export any documents you want to keep using [Export your document](/knowledge-base/export-your-document). Once deletion is processed it cannot be undone.
