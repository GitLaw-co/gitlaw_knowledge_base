You set the jurisdiction and the Agent drafts, reviews, and adapts clauses accordingly. GitLaw ships a searchable list of supported jurisdictions covering countries and their regions - **England & Wales**, **California**, **Singapore** and **United States of America** among them. If yours isn't on the list, name it in your prompt and the Agent will apply the relevant legal context.

### How to set your jurisdiction

There are three ways to control it:

1. **Profile default** - Go to **Settings → Profile** and open **Jurisdiction** to set a default that applies to every new chat and document. Pick from the searchable list of supported jurisdictions.
2. **Per chat** - Open the jurisdiction chip above the message box. It shows the jurisdiction currently in effect, and you can pick another one for that chat. Useful when you're switching between matters in different countries.
3. **In your prompt** - Just name it: _"Draft a mutual NDA governed by English law."_ The Agent picks it up and adjusts automatically, even if your profile is set elsewhere.

For step-by-step instructions, see [Set your jurisdiction](/knowledge-base/set-your-jurisdiction).

### Finding jurisdiction-specific templates

The public template library lets you filter by jurisdiction, so you can browse NDAs, service agreements, and other templates tagged to a specific country or region. Selecting a country (e.g., US) returns templates for that country plus all its regions (e.g., California, Delaware). You can also filter by multiple jurisdictions at once.

To start from a template, see [Create a draft document using the Agent](/knowledge-base/create-a-draft-document-using-agent).

### What if my jurisdiction isn't listed?

If your jurisdiction doesn't appear as a pre-set option, type it directly into your prompt. The Agent will do its best to apply the relevant legal context. For highly specialised or less common jurisdictions, always review the output carefully with a qualified local lawyer.
