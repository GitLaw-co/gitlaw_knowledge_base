No. A colleague you invite only sees the organisation you invited them to. Your personal life stays yours.

## Your worlds are kept separate

Each **organisation** has its own contacts and its own **memory** (the things GitLaw remembers about how you work). They never mix.

- Your personal organisation holds personal contacts - your landlord, your plumber, your dentist.
- Your business organisation holds business contacts.

Invite a colleague into your business organisation and they see that organisation's contacts and memory - nothing else. They never see your personal organisation.

## What about your own preferences?

Your personal preferences - tone of voice, "no emoji", language, theme - are user-level **Settings**, not organisation memory. They travel only with you, and are never shared into a team's memory.

## Who can change shared memory

Within a shared organisation, not everyone can rewrite what it remembers:

| Role   | Shared memory      |
| ------ | ------------------ |
| Owner  | Can read and write |
| Editor | Can read and write |
| Viewer | Read-only          |

In a team, even a confident automatic update to shared memory asks for confirmation first - nothing changes behind your back.

Short version: invite freely. Your personal world and your business world are separate by design.
