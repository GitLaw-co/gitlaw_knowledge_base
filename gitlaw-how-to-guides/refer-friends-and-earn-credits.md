GitLaw's referral programme rewards you for spreading the word. When you invite someone and they join, both you and the person you invited earn **AI credits** - the same credits that power drafting and document review across GitLaw.

## How referrals work

1. **Open the referral or share dialog** from your account. GitLaw gives you a personal invitation link you can share.
2. **Share your link** with friends, colleagues, or clients - by email, message, or anywhere you like.
3. **They sign up** using your link and start using GitLaw.
4. **Both of you earn credits.** Your reward and theirs are added as AI credits.

You can see the credits earned from referrals in your billing settings, under usage.

## Where your credits appear

Referral credits are added to your **AI credit pool** - the same balance you spend on AI drafting and review.

For a full explanation of how credits are measured and spent, see [How does billing work?](/knowledge-base/how-does-billing-work).

## Tips for getting the most from referrals

- Share your link with people who genuinely need contracts drafted or reviewed - they'll get more value, and so will you.
- Both sides benefit, so there's no downside to the person you invite: they start with extra credits too.
- Check **Billing → Usage** to see the referral credits you have earned.

## Quick reference

| Question                          | Answer                                                              |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who earns credits?                | Both you and the person you invite.                                 |
| What do I earn?                   | AI credits, added to your account.                                  |
| Where do the credits go?          | Your personal AI credit balance, including when you are in an org.  |
| How do I invite someone?          | Share your personal invitation link from the referral/share dialog. |
| Where can I see what I've earned? | In billing settings, under usage.                                   |
